## Acoustic Engineering Guide: Sealed vs. Ported Speaker Boxes

When embarking on a custom speaker housing project, your first major decision is choosing the enclosure topology. Both configurations drastically alter how the air spring inside the cabinet interacts with the speaker cone ($V_{as}$).

### 1. Sealed Enclosures (Acoustic Suspension)

Sealed boxes are favored by audiophiles who prioritize transient response, tight accuracy, and a smooth low-end roll-off. Because the air inside the box acts as a perfectly linear dampening spring, power handling is exceptionally stable. Therefore, it is often the best choice for jazz, classical music, critical studio monitoring, and compact spaces.

### 2. Ported Enclosures (Bass Reflex / Vented)

In addition, vented enclosures utilize a tuned column of air inside a port tube to reinforce low-frequency output near the tuning frequency ($F_b$). By aligning a Helmholtz resonator with the driver’s resonant frequency ($F_s$), you gain up to 3dB to 6dB of extra efficiency in the deep bass register.

However, the bass rolls off much faster below the tuning frequency. Specifically, it drops at a rate of 24dB per octave compared to the gentler 12dB slope of sealed variants.

## Choosing the Right Material: MDF vs. Baltic Birch Ply

The structure of your enclosure must be as inert as possible to prevent panel resonance from coloring the sound. According to premium audio manufacturing standards, two materials reign supreme in the industry today.

First, Medium-Density Fiberboard (MDF) is completely isotropic with no internal voids. MDF offers incredible internal acoustic dampening and is easy to router or CNC machine. However, it is remarkably heavy and sensitive to environmental moisture.

Second, Baltic Birch Plywood features multi-layered void-free construction. It is significantly lighter than MDF while offering superior mechanical strength and structural rigidity. Consequently, it holds screws tightly and showcases a gorgeous natural wood grain when varnished.
